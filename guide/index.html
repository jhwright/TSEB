1What this is

For years TSEB's files have lived in two kinds of places: the Groups.io file area, and the personal Google Drives of about a dozen volunteers. When a volunteer steps away, their files can go with them.

The TSEB Library brings those files together in one place that belongs to TSEB, sorted into the folder plan that every circle approved in September 2026. This guide explains how the library works so anyone can find things and help keep it tidy.

This page is public; the library is not. Anyone can read this guide. The links on it open only for people who have been given access to the library.

Have a question or comment? Every section ends with a button like the one below. It opens a page in a shared Google Doc, one page per topic, where you can write your note. Jeff answers there.

3How the library is organized

The library follows the Proposed Outline of TSEB folders, drafted by Margit and approved by every circle in September 2026. Each circle has its own folder. Older material that no current circle owns goes to Archives.

Also at the top of the library

  • _START HERE: a two-minute explanation of the library.
  • _Manifest and descriptions: every file from the first export, one row each, with its old Groups.io note and link.
  • _For Hadas and _Questions and comments on the TSEB Library Guide: the places to answer questions and leave notes.

Files whose names start with an underscore ( _ ) sort to the top, so these are easy to find.

4Who can see and change what

In Google Drive, access is given to folders, not to single files. Whoever can open a folder can open everything inside it, including files added later. That keeps things simple: set each folder once and leave it.

Protocols

  • Links don't open doors. A link to a file works only for someone who already has access to its folder. You can paste library links in emails to members without worry.
  • "Anyone with the link" stays off. That setting opens a file to the whole internet. Most of the old Groups.io files were set this way, which is how they could be copied. The library won't use it.
  • One file, many places: use a shortcut. If a file belongs in two folders, keep the real file in one and put a Drive shortcut in the other. A shortcut never gives access by itself.
  • People outside TSEB (facility staff, presenters) are not added to the library. Send them a copy instead.

Still being weighed

Two ways to manage access as people join and leave circles. Neither is decided; Hadas and Iris will choose.

  • A Google Group per circle (for example, a music-circle address). Share the folder with the group, and a person's access follows when they join or leave.
  • A separate Shared Drive per circle. Very simple permissions, but harder to search across circles and awkward to move files between them. A middle path: one library for everything, plus one small separate drive only for the most sensitive material.

5How a member's files get added

Hadas's Groups.io files were the first set. The same steps will be used for each of the other members.

The steps in words

  1. Gather. The member sends a Groups.io export, or shares their TSEB folder from their own Drive with Jeff (viewing is enough).
  2. Upload. An export is unzipped and dragged into Google Drive. Word and Excel files turn into Google Docs and Sheets on the way in.
  3. Sort. An AI helper reads each file's name and its old Groups.io note and proposes a folder in the outline, with a reason. It marks anything it is unsure of.
  4. Place. Files are moved into their folders. Google Docs that Groups.io only linked to are copied in, so they no longer depend on one person's account. Short Groups.io notes are added to the file name in brackets.
  5. Review. The member gets a short Google Doc named "_For their name" with questions, duplicates and anything that couldn't be copied. They write their answers under each item.
  6. Settle. Their answers are carried out, and anything unresolved is listed in Open questions below.
Originals are never changed. Groups.io and each member's own Drive stay exactly as they were, so any step can be undone.

6Naming and tidying rules

  • Put the date first, as year, month, day. "2025.10.30 Business Meeting Minutes" lines up in order. "Minutes Oct 30" and "Biz notes final" do not. agreed
  • Let the folder say what is current. Words like "updated", "corrected", "v3" and "final" stop being true when the next version appears. Keep the live file in a Current folder and move the old one to Archive. agreed
  • One copy of each file. When two copies turn up, keep the newer or approved one and remove the other. If unsure, ask the owner.
  • PDFs can stay PDFs. The roster, for example, stays a PDF so only its keeper can change it.
  • Word templates. A few outreach letters are meant to be downloaded and edited in Word. Any Google Doc can be saved as Word with File → Download → Microsoft Word. Whether to also keep the original Word files is an open question.
  • Folder notes. Like Groups.io, Google Drive folders have a description you can fill in (right-click the folder, then Folder information or Details).

7Sensitive material

Bedside singing reports may mention patients, and TSEB abides by HIPAA. They belong to the Music Circle and are written by the bedside coordinator. For now they are held outside the shared library, in a folder only Jeff can open, until the coordinator decides who may read them. Nobody read their contents while building the library, and they are kept out of any AI work.

Protocols

  • Bedside reports belong in Music Circle → Bedside singing reports, in a folder limited to the people the coordinator chooses. The bedside coordinator decides who may read them. A version shared at a business meeting should have patient details removed.
  • Rosters and member lists are the member directory: members may view, only the roster keeper changes them, and past rosters are kept as history.
  • Passwords and logins don't belong in meeting notes. One old set of minutes contains a login for a retired website; it should be blanked out.
  • Watch inherited access. Every folder inside a shared folder is shared too. A restricted folder must sit outside the shared area, or be set to limited access once the library is in TSEB's Workspace.
  • Nothing sensitive goes into an AI tool unless the people responsible have agreed. An AI service is not covered by Google's health-privacy agreement by default.

8AI summaries of meeting notes

Meeting notes are the largest part of the library, about 90 files in many styles. To make them easier to search, each one now has a short summary written by AI.

  • Google Doc minutes: the summary sits in a shaded box at the very top of the document.
  • PDF minutes: a Google Doc sits beside each PDF, named "… (text + AI summary)". It has the summary on top and the full text of the PDF below. The PDF itself is unchanged.
  • Every summary lists topics discussed and decisions made, and is clearly marked as written by AI on a given date. The original notes are never changed.
  • For a group that keeps all its notes in one running document, the summary gives one line per meeting.
  • Bedside reports are never summarized.
Summaries can contain mistakes. When something matters, check the original notes below the summary.

9Using AI helpers

AI tools did the heavy sorting for the first set of files, and can answer questions like "when did we decide on rehearsal stipends?" by reading the library.

Which tools

  • Claude (from Anthropic) sorted the files, copied the linked documents and wrote the summaries. It can move many files at once.
  • Gemini comes built into Google Workspace. It is handy for everyday questions, but in testing it could not sort or move files in bulk.

Rules for any AI helper

  • It works only through the access of the person using it, and never shares or deletes anything on its own.
  • It labels anything it writes as AI-generated, with the date.
  • It stays out of bedside reports and doesn't copy rosters into other places.
  • It proposes; a person decides.

An idea under discussion

Keep these rules, the folder outline and the step-by-step recipe as ordinary Google Docs in a small "About this library" folder. Any AI that can read the library would then follow the same rules. Only admins would be able to edit that folder, because AI tools follow written instructions.

13Words we use

Google Drive
Google's online storage for documents, spreadsheets, PDFs, audio and more.
My Drive
A person's own area in Google Drive. Files there belong to that person.
Google Workspace
Google's service for organizations. TSEB is getting it free as a nonprofit. It gives TSEB its own accounts and storage.
Shared Drive
Storage owned by the organization rather than a person. Files stay even when people leave.
Folder access
Permission to open a folder and everything in it. Levels are view, comment, edit and manage.
"Anyone with the link"
A sharing setting that lets anyone on the internet open a file if they have its link. The library doesn't use it.
Shortcut
A pointer to a file, placed in a second folder. Opening it opens the real file. It doesn't grant access.
Google Group
A named list of people with one email address. Folders can be shared with a group instead of with each person.
Groups.io
The email-group service TSEB uses, which also has a file area.
Export
A download of everything in the Groups.io file area, as one zip file.
Sidecar file
A small ".json" file that came with each item in the export, holding its Groups.io note, dates and link. Used for sorting, then set aside.
Manifest
A spreadsheet listing every file that was added, where it went, and its original note and link.
Companion doc
A Google Doc placed beside a PDF, holding the PDF's text and an AI summary.
AI summary
A short list of topics and decisions written by an AI tool, labelled as such, at the top of meeting notes.
AI helper
A tool such as Claude or Gemini that can read and organize files for the person using it.
Connector
The link that lets an AI helper reach a service like Google Drive, using the person's own sign-in.
HIPAA
A U.S. law protecting people's health information.
Circle
A group responsible for one area of TSEB's work: Leadership (TLC), Communications, Finance, Membership or Music.
TLC
Threshold Leadership Circle.
Archives
Folder H, for older material that no current circle owns, such as business meetings before 2026.
